When the simulation lies

Designing a motor speed controller that worked perfectly in simulation, then making it work on real hardware.

When
Advanced Control and Measurement, 2025
My role
Individual project
Tools
MATLAB, Simulink, ESP32

Start with the data

The motor was a black box: no datasheet model, just its behaviour. I logged six datasets from an ESP32 and used system identification to fit a model. Higher-order models scored better on the data they were trained on, but a second-order model did best on data it had never seen, with a 93% fit. I chose that one. A model that only explains its training data is not much use.

Two controllers, both perfect on screen

I designed two controllers in MATLAB and Simulink: an auto-tuned PID and a lead compensator designed with root locus. In simulation both were fast, stable and shrugged off injected noise and disturbances.

Then the hardware

On the real motor, both oscillated, with overshoot of about 50%. The model had missed something. Tracing the loop, I found a delay between measuring speed and acting on it that the identified model never included. Small delays like this destabilise controllers that look fine on paper.

The fix

I added the delay to the design model and re-tuned. The final lead compensator, running on the ESP32, reached its target speed in 0.07 s, settled in 0.86 s and held within 0.01% of the setpoint, and it rejected load disturbances on the shaft.
